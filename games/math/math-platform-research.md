# Math Platform Research — Top 10 Educational Math Games

> Research date: 2026-08-27  
> Purpose: Identify proven gameplay features from leading educational math platforms and adapt them to the Sahara Play / Aldoolab math section, with an Omani cultural voice.

## 2. Top 20 Gameplay-Zone Features

> Focused on the active play area (not menus, onboarding, or dashboards). Ranked by suitability for Sahara Play's target age (7–12) and cultural fit.

| Rank | Feature | Why It Works | Omani Enhancement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Timed answer rewards** | Builds fluency; creates urgency without pressure | *Hourglass shaped like a brass dallah*; fast answers earn "golden dates" |
| 2 | **Combo chains** | Encourages accuracy streaks; highly motivating | *Combo flame becomes a line of frankincense smoke*; ×5 triggers a guardian roar |
| 3 | **Visual problem presentation** | Reduces reading load; supports Arabic numerals | *Problems appear on stone tablets, clay tags, or woven souq banners* |
| 4 | **Immediate haptic/audio feedback** | Reinforces correct/wrong instantly | *Correct = oud pluck + coin clink; wrong = pottery crack + low drum* |
| 5 | **Progressive difficulty within a level** | Keeps players in flow | *Corridor gets darker / customers get more impatient / dhow sails farther* |
| 6 | **Character/avatar progression** | Long-term motivation | *Dress the young guard in different dishdashas, kumahs, or badges* |
| 7 | **Pet/companion helper** | Emotional attachment; can give hints | *Falcon companion that scouts ahead or brings a hint scroll* |
| 8 | **Boss encounters** | Breaks repetition; tests mixed skills | *Boss = greedy merchant or sandstorm spirit with multi-step riddles* |
| 9 | **Power-ups tied to correct answers** | Rewards skill with temporary aid | *Time lamp, extra sand burst, treasure lock, frankincense smoke shield* |
| 10 | **Collectible currency/rewards** | Tangible sense of progress | *Omani baisa, silver coins, frankincense resin, pearl shells* |
| 11 | **Daily challenge / streak** | Habit formation | *Daily caravan arrives at the souq with a special order* |
| 12 | **Local co-op / hotseat** | Social play in Majlis setting | *Two players trade at the same stall or guard alternating corridors* |
| 13 | **Real-world context word problems** | Shows math utility | *Prices in riyals/baisa, distances between Omani cities, weights of dates* |
| 14 | **Physical manipulation of numbers** | Concrete → abstract learning | *Drag coins to make change; slide number tiles on a palm-wood abacus* |
| 15 | **Level stars + unlock paths** | Clear goals and replay value | *1–3 stars unlock new souq stalls or fort corridors* |
| 16 | **Mini-map / world map** | Sense of journey | *Map of Oman wilayats; each region unlocks a new math topic* |
| 17 | **Hint system with small cost** | Prevents frustration | *Falcon companion fetches a hint but takes a few seconds / points* |
| 18 | **Wrong-answer scaffolding** | Learning from mistakes | *Show the correct step using Omani bar models or number-line carpets* |
| 19 | **Endless / practice mode** | Low-stakes repetition | *Endless souq day or endless guard duty* |
| 20 | **Short celebratory animations** | Positive reinforcement | *Victory: vault door glows, customer dances, dhow fires a confetti cannon* |

## 3. Cultural Anchors Summary

Recurring Omani elements that can thread through all math games:

- **Settings:** hill fort corridors, Muttrah souq stalls, Wahiba dunes, Muscat harbor, date palm oasis, frankincense trail, Jebel Akhdar terraces.
- **Objects:** khanjar, dallah, brass scales, clay jars, date baskets, frankincense burners, abacus, astrolabe, dhow model, pearl shells.
- **Sounds:** oud/taqsim background, copper coin clink, pottery thud, desert wind, market chatter, adhan-inspired melodic cues.
- **Characters:** young fort guardian, clever souq merchant, falconer, sailor, date farmer, elder storyteller.
- **Progress tokens:** silver coins, baisa, frankincense resin, pearls, stamps for each wilayat.

## 4. Design Principles Derived

1. **Math first, theme second.** Every interaction must solve a real curriculum problem; the Omani theme is the wrapper, not the content.
2. **One-handed, 64 px+ touch targets.** Tablet-first; many children use shared family devices.
3. **No failure shame.** Wrong answers advance the challenge but always offer a path back (hint, slower pace, practice mode).
4. **Procedural audio only.** No licensed music or voice files; keep build small and offline-capable.
5. **Session length 2–4 minutes.** Match school break lengths and attention spans.
6. **Arabic-first UI.** Numerals, labels, and feedback in Arabic; English available for mixed learners.
